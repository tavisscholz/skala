Field note 01

Your best manager cannot be the whole system.

A strong manager can make a weak process look workable. They remember the exceptions, catch the missed handoffs, and answer the same questions every week.

That knowledge matters. The problem starts when the business can only use it while that manager is in the room.

Start with one task that keeps coming back. Watch how the manager handles it. Write down the decisions that matter, give the next person a usable reference, and test it during a real shift.

A useful standard carries judgment forward. It gives the next manager a better place to start and leaves your strongest people more time to lead.

Field note 02

The next opening starts in the stores you already have.

An opening plan can be busy and still miss the work that matters. Fixtures arrive. Hiring happens. The calendar fills up. But the new manager may still be learning a process that nobody has made clear.

Before the next opening, look at what the current stores already struggle to do consistently. Choose the issues that would be costly to carry into another location.

Give each one an owner. Build the missing reference or routine. Test it with people who were not involved in creating it.

Expansion becomes more useful when every opening carries forward what the business has learned. The next store should inherit experience, not the same avoidable confusion.
