# Make Sure Your Opening Checklist Covers This

2026-09-16

*The dates and handoffs that should never surprise the team.*

**Operator Tool**

The final inspection for the occupancy permit gets pushed two and a half weeks out. Nobody dropped the ball on purpose. The fire marshal came through on the first pass, found a handful of corrective items before he'd sign off (a couple of exit signs, a sprinkler head clearance issue, an alarm zone that didn't test clean) and had to schedule a return visit. That part is routine. What isn't routine is that four days passed before anyone outside construction knew about it, because nobody was treating a held inspection as urgent information that had to move immediately.

That's not really a fire marshal problem, and it's not really a construction problem either. It's a communication gap wearing a scheduling problem's clothes. A failed or held-up inspection is information, and information sitting in one person's inbox for four days is functionally the same as no information at all. Real estate and ops were still planning around the original inspection date because nobody told them it had slipped, so by the time they adjusted staffing, training, and the marketing spend, they were reacting instead of managing.

The fix is still one document, one owner per line, tracked from lease execution through grand opening, plus a rule sitting on top of it: any inspection that doesn't clear clean gets flagged to every downstream owner the same day, not once the punch list is resolved. Not a project plan with 200 tasks nobody looks at again after week one. Just the dates that, if missed, cause real damage: a blown labor plan, a marketing push that launches into an empty store, or a certificate of occupancy that lands later than the lease requires.

## A starting template

| Milestone | Owner |
| --- | --- |
| Lease execution date | Real Estate |
| Permit submission date | Construction |
| Utility turn-on request (30+ days out) | Construction |
| Rough construction milestone (framing/MEP complete) | Construction |
| Health department pre-inspection walkthrough | Construction, confirmed by Ops |
| Final health/fire/building inspection scheduled | Construction |
| Escalation on any failed or held inspection result (same day) | Whoever received the result |
| POS and back-office systems install date | IT/Ops |
| Store manager hire date (target: 6-8 weeks pre-open) | Ops/HR |
| Staff hiring complete | Store Manager |
| Training start date | Ops |
| Inventory/first order delivery date | Ops |
| Soft open date | Ops |
| Grand opening / marketing launch date | Marketing |

The column that actually prevents surprises isn't the date, it's the named owner, and the rule that a held inspection gets reported the same day is what keeps a routine correction from turning into a two-week slip. Build five to seven days of buffer into your target inspection date. Fire marshals get backed up, punch lists happen, and a schedule with no room for either isn't a schedule, it's a hope.

**This week:** Pull your last three store openings. For each one, check whether the final CO or fire inspection cleared on the first pass. If it didn't, find out how many days passed between the failed result and the moment real estate, ops, or marketing found out about it. That gap, not the fire marshal's calendar, is what actually cost you time.
