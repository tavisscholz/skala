# How Your Item 19 Will Break Your Sales Pipeline

2026-09-16

*Capital tells you who can fund it. It doesn't tell you who can run it.*

**Field Note**

A candidate clears every number on the sheet. Net worth well above the minimum, liquid capital in hand, financing pre-approved before the first discovery call even wraps. The franchise sales team is thrilled, because financial qualification is the easiest box to check and the fastest way to move a pipeline forward. Eighteen months later, that same location is one of the ones you're quietly worried about, not because the franchisee ran out of money, but because they never should have been running a store day to day in the first place.

Here's what's actually happening. Financial qualification measures one thing: whether someone can fund the investment. It says nothing about whether they can hire and manage hourly labor, hold a team accountable on a bad Saturday, or grind through a rough first year without checking out mentally. I've watched franchise sales teams get excited about a strong balance sheet, only to watch that same franchisee flounder eight months in, not from a cash problem, but because they'd never actually managed a team before. Development teams collapse "qualified" into the capital number because it's the easiest thing to verify with a document. It becomes a proxy for readiness, and proxies get treated as the whole answer, especially when the pipeline is thin and everyone wants to hit a signing number this quarter.

The fix isn't harder math. It's a second screen that runs in parallel with financial qualification, not stacked underneath it where it gets skipped the moment someone shows up well-capitalized. That screen should test for operating fit specifically: hands-on management style versus absentee-investor intent, real experience hiring and managing hourly staff, and how they've actually handled a hard stretch in a previous business or team-management role, not how they say they'd handle it. Validation calls should ask existing franchisees behavior-based questions too, not "would you do this again," but "walk me through the worst week you've had and what you did about it."

**This week:** Pull your last five to ten approved franchisees. Sort them into two piles, thriving operationally and struggling operationally, regardless of how their P&L looks. For each one, look back at what you actually knew about their operating background at the time you approved them. If the split doesn't line up with anything you screened for, that's the gap in your qualification process, and it's costing you more than a bad site ever would.
